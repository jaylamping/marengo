## Connectors and pinout

- **Board port protection (rev A schematic):** electronic trip requested at 15 A (the real overload limit for XT30 contacts and 16 AWG), with a 20 A 58 V fuse as the backstop if the switch fails short.
- **Board port:** the board has no room for a plug, so each arm port is a short pigtail (ring lugs + CAN header on the board) ending in an inline XT30(2+2)-F; the trunk starts with XT30(2+2)-M (power board design README 1a, main 9ce67a0).
- **Joint breaks (recommended):** XT30 pair for power with the **female on the upstream (live) side**, plus a JST-GH 2-pin latching pair for CAN. Four breaks: J1 to J4.
- **RS02 / RS00 plug:** AMASS XT30(2+2)-F.G.B. VBAT+ and GND on the blades; CAN-L and CAN-H on the signal pins (vendor diagram: CAN-L above CAN-H, beside GND).
- **RS03 plugs:** AMASS XT30UW-F (VBAT+, GND) and GH1.25-T 2-pin (CAN-H, CAN-L per vendor diagram).
- Confirm every pin position against the drive in hand before soldering; the vendor gives the pinout as a photo ([../../docs/robstride/README.md](../../../docs/robstride/README.md)).

## Splices, termination, protection

- Drop splice: strip a window in each trunk conductor, wrap the drop conductor, solder, heat-shrink each, then an overall shrink. Stagger the four splices ~10 mm apart so the lump stays thin (~6 mm across).
- Termination: 120 Ω ¼ W across CAN_H and CAN_L at the M15 splice, heat-shrunk; the other end is the board's switchable 120 Ω. Powered off with everything plugged in, CAN_H to CAN_L should read ~60 Ω.
- **Accepted risk (pending Joey's sign-off): bus voltage onto CAN at the arm.** If a crushed or miswired trunk puts VBUS (up to 54.6 V) onto CAN_H or CAN_L, the only protection on the arm side is each drive's own CAN transceiver, and RobStride does not publish its fault rating. A cheap splice clamp cannot help: a 24 V-class CAN TVS dies at 54 V, and a 58 V-class one clamps above the bus anyway. Mitigations: CAN pair in its own sleeve inside the trunk, keyed and latching connectors only, plug and unplug with the E-stop open, abrasion protection at every edge and joint, and the continuity/short check before every first power-up after rework.
- No TVS at the splices: a 24 V-class CAN TVS (NUP2105L) dies at 54 V. CAN surge protection is on the power board port (SMAJ58CA + 58 V-tolerant isolated transceivers).
- Plug and unplug only with the E-stop open.

## Checks after building

1. Continuity on every conductor end to end, including through each joint break; no short between any pair.
2. CAN_H to CAN_L about 60 Ω with both terminators in.
3. Before fitting to the arm, move each joint by hand through full range with the harness in place: no pull, pinch or rub; note how much slack each crossing used and trim the cut list.
4. First power-up per the bench harness doc (3 A fuse, one motor at a time).
5. **Backdrive (from the vendor manuals, section 4.2.5 and the parameter tables; described in the RS00 and RS03 manuals; the RS02 manual lists the same `damper` parameter (0x702A, default 0) without the description, so confirm it with a hand-spin on an unpowered RS02):** each drive has a *post-power-off anti-backdrive protection*. When the drive is unpowered and spun fast by hand or by a fall, it applies damping so the motor cannot generate a voltage surge. It is **on by default** (`damper` = 0, parameter 0x702A / 0x2026 on RS03; set to 1 to disable). So an unpowered drive, including one isolated by a blown drop fuse, protects itself. **Rule: never set `damper` = 1** (flag for the code project and the bench config). The manual also warns that a *powered* drive in damping mode "generates electricity ... and requires power supply to prevent overvoltage": powered braking still needs the BMS and clamp path below. Drive overvoltage fault trips at 60 V; undervoltage at 12 V; each drive reports its own VBUS over CAN. Bench check: with a drive unpowered, spin it by hand and feel the damping, to confirm the feature on our firmware.

**Regen path (Joey 2026-11-06):** braking energy goes back through the BMS into the pack first (CHG FET on while running, 53.9 V charge stop for headroom); the clamp only takes overflow.
