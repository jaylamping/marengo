   - **Force and stress at $17\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ peak motor torque:**
     $$F_{\text{stop}} = \frac{17.0\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}}{0.03825\text{ m}} = 444.4\text{ N}, \qquad \sigma_{\text{contact,avg}} = \frac{444.4\text{ N}}{22.75\text{ mm}^2} = 19.54\text{ MPa}$$
     Against the assumed $45\text{ MPa}$ compressive/tensile limit, uniform bearing SF is $45 / 19.54 = 2.30$ (barely clearing 2.0 before any edge-loading knockdown from the $1.25\text{–}3.3\text{ mm}$ out-of-plane deflection).

The $2^\circ$ extension (`FINGER_PAD_133`, $\theta = 61.0^\circ\text{ to }63.0^\circ$, arc length $1.33\text{ mm}$) is cantilevered circumferentially at $r = 35.0\text{ to }41.5\text{ mm}$ with no inner radial web from $\theta = 61^\circ\text{ to }63^\circ$.

### Q3. Do the worst FEA results survive scrutiny? (Upper arm layer SF 1.76 vs 1.33; Fork SF 2.26 vs 2.07)

1. **Upper arm layer SF (`1.33` in Pass 1 $\to$ `1.76` in Pass 2 on LC1A) does NOT survive scrutiny:**
   - **Why it moved:** In Pass 2, the hoop (`X = 151.70 to 165.19 mm`, center $X = 158.45\text{ mm}$) and collar (`X = 165.19 to 192.19 mm`) moved $+4.0\text{ mm}$ closer to the upper-arm tube axis ($X = 170.49\text{ mm}$), reducing the lateral eccentricity $\Delta X$ from $16.05\text{ mm}$ to $12.05\text{ mm}$ ($-25\%$). For a $300\text{ N}$ fore-aft load ($F_z$ in LC1A/LC1B), that reduces the secondary out-of-plane moment $M_y = F_z \cdot \Delta X$ peeling the hoop in $+X$.
   - **Why `1.76` is an unconverged mesh artifact:**
     1. In linear elasticity, `LC1B = -LC1A` ([BASELINE.md](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/data/BASELINE.md#L90)), so $\sigma_{xx}^{\text{LC1B}}(\mathbf{p}) = -\sigma_{xx}^{\text{LC1A}}(\mathbf{p})$ everywhere. Yet between Pass 1 and Pass 2, the overall von Mises SF barely moved ($2.18 \to 2.21$, $+1.4\%$), while `LC1A` layer SF jumped $+32\%$ ($1.33 \to 1.76$, $\sigma_{xx} = 16.9 \to 12.8\text{ MPa}$) and `LC1B` layer SF dropped $-19\%$ ($2.75 \to 2.22$, $\sigma_{xx} = 8.2 \to 10.1\text{ MPa}$) at the rigid-linked M3 hole edge.
     2. In [BASELINE.md](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/data/BASELINE.md#L39-L44), refining the exact same rigid-bolt M3 hole from a $3\text{ mm}$ mesh to a $2\text{ mm}$ or $0.8\text{ mm}$ hole-refined mesh caused the layer SF to drop from **`2.13` ($10.6\text{ MPa}$) down to `1.28` ($17.6\text{ MPa}$, $-40\%$) and `1.39` ($16.2\text{ MPa}$, $-35\%$)**. Even re-running the identical Rev B $3\text{ mm}$ model in [ELBOW_COMPACTION.md](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/data/ELBOW_COMPACTION.md#L155) shifted SF from $2.15$ to $2.02$.
     3. No mesh convergence or $0.8\text{ mm}$ hole refinement was run on Pass 2 ([PACKET.md](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/PACKET.md#L29)). Applying the [BASELINE.md](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/data/BASELINE.md#L39-L44) refinement ratio ($0.60\times\text{ to }0.65\times$) to Pass 2's $3\text{ mm}$-mesh layer SF of $1.76$ projects a refined layer SF of **$1.06\text{ to }1.15$ — still failing the $1.50$ target**.

2. **Fork SF (`2.07` in Pass 1 $\to$ `1.94` shift-only $\to$ `2.26` in Pass 2, layer SF `1.61`) — model changes are NOT fair:**
   - **Why it moved:** Shifting the fork cheeks $+4\text{ mm}$ outboard while leaving the `Ø64 mm` forearm tube on $X = 170.49\text{ mm}$ increased the outboard cheek overhang past the tube from $0.7\text{ mm}$ to $4.7\text{ mm}$ ([p2_fork_section.png](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/views/p2_fork_section.png)), dropping twist SF to $1.94$ at the outboard gusset notch ($X = 196\text{–}197, Y = 150$). Extending `GUSSET_OUT` to the full $36.5\text{ mm}$ depth ($Z = -26.014\text{ to }+10.486\text{ mm}$, [p2_gusset.py](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/scripts/p2_gusset.py)) closed that notch and raised the 3 mm-mesh von Mises SF to $2.26$, while `+X` layer SF stayed stagnant ($1.60 \to 1.61$).
   - **Four reasons the Fork FEA model ([FOREARM_FEA_VM.png](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/views/FOREARM_FEA_VM.png)) is unconservative/unfair:**

### Q4. Load path table, shorter bearing span trade, and whether printed parts fail before the actuator

1. **Independent verification of the load path arithmetic ([ELBOW_COMPACTION.md](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/data/ELBOW_COMPACTION.md#L21-L23)):**
   - **6808 bearing center $X_{6808}$:** Before $= (124.7 + 131.7)/2 = 128.20\text{ mm}$; Pass 1 $= (139.0 + 146.0)/2 = 142.50\text{ mm}$; Pass 2 $= (143.0 + 150.0)/2 = 146.50\text{ mm}$.
   - **RS02 output face $X_{\text{face}}$:** Before / Pass 1 $= 193.14\text{ mm}$ ($193.19\text{ mm}$ in CAD); Pass 2 $= 197.14\text{ mm}$ ($197.19\text{ mm}$ in CAD).
   - **Span to RS02 output face:** Before $= 193.14 - 128.20 = \mathbf{64.94\text{ mm}}$; Pass 1 & Pass 2 $= 197.14 - 146.50 = \mathbf{50.64\text{ mm}}$ ($-22.0\%$).
   - **Couple from $M = 19.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ ($100\text{ N} \times 0.195\text{ m}$):** Before $= 19.5 / 0.06494 = \mathbf{300.3\text{ N}}$; Pass 1 & 2 $= 19.5 / 0.05064 = \mathbf{385.1\text{ N}}$ ($+28.2\%$).
   - **RS02 share of a transverse load along $X_{\text{arm}} = 170.49\text{ mm}$:**
     - Before: $(170.49 - 128.20)/64.94 = 42.29 / 64.94 = \mathbf{65.1\%}$.
     - Pass 1: $(170.49 - 142.50)/50.64 = 27.99 / 50.64 = \mathbf{55.3\%}$.
     - Pass 2: $(170.49 - 146.50)/50.64 = 23.99 / 50.64 = \mathbf{47.4\%}$.

2. **What the table hides:**
   - **True bearing span is ~44.6 mm, not 50.6 mm:** The RS02's internal output bearing sits $\sim 5\text{–}7\text{ mm}$ inboard of the output flange face ($X_{\text{brg}} \approx 191.1\text{ mm}$ in Pass 2). Using $X_{\text{brg}} \approx 191.14\text{ mm}$ gives a true bearing span of **$44.64\text{ mm}$** (down $24\%$ from $58.94\text{ mm}$), raising the $19.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ couple to **$437\text{ N}$** ($+45\%$) and the RS02 radial load share to **$53.7\%$** (not $47\%$).
   - **Hand-tip lever arm ($285\text{ mm}$ vs $195\text{ mm}$) and simultaneous $14\text{–}17\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ yaw twist ($M_y$):** A $100\text{ N}$ lateral load at the hand tip ($L = 195 + 90 = 285\text{ mm}$) produces $M_z = 28.5\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ $\implies$ **$563\text{ N}$** couple ($638\text{ N}$ on a $44.6\text{ mm}$ span). Simultaneously, a $14\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ twist about the forearm axis $Y$ is reacted across the exact same $50.6\text{ mm}$ bearing span as a fore-aft couple of $14 / 0.05064 = \mathbf{276\text{ N}}$ ($314\text{ N}$ on $44.6\text{ mm}$).
   - **100% of lateral force $F_x$ is axial thrust on the RS02:** Because $X$ is the elbow axis, a lateral force $F_x = 100\text{ N}$ (or $300\text{ N}$ in `LC1C`) is **axial** to the elbow; if the 6808 floats, $100\%$ of $F_x$ goes axially into the RS02 output bearing in addition to the radial couple.

3. **Is the shorter span the right trade, and do printed parts fail before the actuator?**
   - **No.** Nesting the bearing saved only **$3.0\text{ mm}$** of stack width ($78.5 \to 75.5\text{ mm}$) — all of which is lost once the $8\text{–}9\text{ mm}$ elbow jumper is routed inboard of the cheek ($X = 121.7\text{ to }131.2\text{ mm}$, total width $85.5\text{ mm}$) — while increasing side-moment bearing forces by $+28\%$ to $+45\%$.
   - **"Printed parts fail before the actuator" is violated or unproven:**
     1. No RobStride RS02 bearing rating exists ([PACKET.md](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/PACKET.md#L29)).
     2. By beefing up the upper arm ($SF = 2.17$ at $300\text{ N}$ lateral $\implies$ yields above $650\text{ N}$ / $140\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$) and the fork ($SF = 2.26$ at $100\text{ N}$ lateral + $14\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$ twist $\implies$ yields above $226\text{ N}$ / $44\text{ N}\cdot\text{m}$, putting $>870\text{ N}$ couple on the bearings before link yield), the links are now strong enough to overload the RS02 output bearing before the tubes break.

### Q6. Printability of fork and carrier; `+X` vs `+Y` vs a better third orientation

1. **Carrier (`C2_PILOT`) is printed upside-down ("Outboard face up" / $+X$ up):**
   - Putting $X = 140.50\text{ mm}$ on the build plate places the **bearing floor ($X = 150.00\text{ mm}$, $r = 21.0\text{ to }26.0\text{ mm}$)** $9.5\text{ mm}$ up in the air over an open `Ø52 mm` hole—an unbridgeable annular overhang around a central `Ø42 mm` void that requires supports on the precision bearing seat. It also puts the $173^\circ$ open-rim stop recess ($X = 144.50\text{ mm}$, which is a $7.5\text{ mm}$ radial cantilever, **not** a bridge because the outer rim at $r = 42\text{ mm}$ is open air), all 3 washer pockets ($X = 143.00\text{ mm}$), and all 6 M3 counterbores ($X = 146.70\text{ mm}$) face-down on supports.

In `-X` up, the `Ø52 mm` bearing seat floor, the stop recess, the 3 washer pockets, and the 6 M3 counterbores all face **upward** and print with **zero supports**.

2. **Fork (`FINGER_PAD_133`) in `+X` vs `+Y` vs `+Z` (better third orientation):**
   - **Why `+Y` fails:** Layers are $Y = \text{const}$ (perpendicular to the forearm axis), so primary axial bending tension $\sigma_{yy}$ pulls directly across the $22.5\text{ MPa}$ weak layer interface at the inboard gusset root ($\text{layer SF} = 0.82$).
   - **Why `+X` is poor:**
     1. The entire inner face of the outboard cheek (**$X = 197.19\text{ mm}$**, the precision mating face against the RS02 output flange, including the 3 × `Ø4 mm` dowel holes and 6 × `Ø4.5 mm` M4 holes) is a **$4,112\text{ mm}^2$ ($4,553\text{ mm}^2$ total flat down-facing) horizontal overhang ceiling** printed $65.5\text{ mm}$ in the air on tree supports across a $47.3\text{ mm}$ slot.
     2. The `Ø40 mm` bearing stub ($X = 142.8\text{ to }149.9\text{ mm}$) and stop finger ($X = 139.7\text{ to }144.0\text{ mm}$) are printed with layers perpendicular to $X$, placing the $R = 0$ steps at $X = 139.7\text{ mm}$ and $142.8\text{ mm}$ in direct inter-layer shear under bearing and stop loads.
     3. [slice_fork.py](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/scripts/slice_fork.py#L46) forces `sparse_infill_density = 100%` with `zig-zag` solid infill ($197.7\text{ g}$), which in PETG HF causes severe thermal warpage and nozzle over-extrusion accumulation on thick solid sections.
   - **Better third orientation — `+Z` up (rear flat $Z = -26.014\text{ mm}$ on the build plate):**
     - In [marengo_forearm_left.step](file:///Users/joseph/scratch/revc2/hardware/designs/left-arm/upper-arm/review/revc2/data/marengo_forearm_left.step), the rear faces of both gussets (`Face 2566`) and the rear tangent of the `Ø64 mm` forearm tube all lie on the plane **$Z = -26.014\text{ mm}$**, giving $1,373\text{ mm}^2$ of direct bed contact out of the box (and even more if the rear tangent is flattened by $0.5\text{ mm}$), with a lower print height (**$69.0\text{ mm}$** vs $75.5\text{ mm}$).
     - **Flat down-facing overhang drops by $78\%$** (from **$4,553\text{ mm}^2$** in $+X$ to **$996\text{ mm}^2$** in $+Z$).
     - **Zero supports on precision mating faces:** Both cheek faces ($X = 131.70, 139.70, 197.19, 203.19, 207.19\text{ mm}$) are **vertical walls** (`X = const`).
     - **Structural advantage of `+Z` up:** Print layers lie in the **$X\text{-}Y$ plane**, so **both** longitudinal tube bending ($\sigma_{yy}$, which killed $+Y$) **and** lateral cheek/gusset-root bending ($\sigma_{xx}$, which limited $+X$ to $1.61$) run **in-layer** ($45\text{ MPa}$ filament direction), while continuous $X\text{-}Y$ perimeter loops run uninterrupted from the inboard cheek through the `Ø40 mm` bearing stub and into the stop finger. And on the flat front ($Z = +10.49\text{ mm}$) and back ($Z = -26.01\text{ mm}$) gusset surfaces where peak notch stresses occur, the surface normal is $\pm Z$ (traction-free), so inter-layer normal tension $\sigma_{zz} = 0$ on those faces.
